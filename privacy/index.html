    Privacy¶

    Last updated 2026-10-27. The history of this page is the git log of docs/privacy.md.

    Scope¶

    This page covers the cointoss instances this project runs: int today, and prod once it runs. cointoss is open source, and anyone can run their own instance. A self-hosted instance is the responsibility of whoever runs it, and this page does not describe it.

    cointoss is served by woodglue, which handles sign-in, sessions and access control. What follows describes what woodglue and cointoss do, as checked against their code and the deployment.

    Who can reach an instance¶

    An instance is reachable only inside the operator's tailnet, the private network Tailscale provides. It is not on the public internet. Guests are invited to the tailnet and granted access to the instance and nothing else on it, so Tailscale also holds their tailnet identity (see Sharing).

    What is collected at sign-in, and why¶

    You sign in with an account you already have. The instance asks the provider only for what it needs to know who you are and whether to let you in.

    • Google (scopes openid email profile): your Google account id, your name, and your email address with whether Google has verified it.
    • GitHub (scopes read:user user:email): your GitHub account id, your name (or your login if you have not set a name), and your primary email if it is verified. If an instance's rules ever refer to GitHub organizations or teams, it also asks for read:org and reads your organization and team memberships.

    Your identity is checked against the instance's admission rules, and the matching roles are assigned. That is the only use of this information: deciding whether you may sign in and what you may do once you have.

    The instances admit anyone who reaches them and signs in, with no roles. Your first sign-in creates your account record (name, verified email, and the linked provider account) and a session, but gives no access to any data until the operator approves the account by giving it a role, or disables it. The record stays while it is pending, and after it is disabled. A sign-in that an instance's admission rules refuse is turned away before any record is created; only the log line described under Logs is kept.

    What is stored¶

    woodglue keeps accounts in a database on the instance's host, auth.db:

    • Your name, your email if the provider verified it, and the provider and account id it is linked to. An unverified email is not stored.
    • Your role assignments.
    • Your sessions and API tokens, each with when it was created, when it was last used and when it expires. A session ends after 12 hours without use or 7 days after sign-in, whichever comes first.

    The provider's access token is used only during sign-in, to read the details above, and is not stored. Organization and team memberships are not stored either; only the roles they lead to are.

    Cookies¶

    • wgl_session: your session. It is HttpOnly, so no page script can read it, and is sent only over HTTPS when the instance is served over HTTPS.
    • wgl_login: set when you start signing in and cleared when the provider sends you back. It ties the sign-in to your browser.
    • _streamlit_xsrf: set by the Dataset explorer (Streamlit) to protect its forms against cross-site requests.

    There are no analytics or advertising cookies, and no trackers. Streamlit's usage statistics are turned off (--browser.gatherUsageStats=false).

    One third-party request remains: woodglue's console page (/console/) loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so opening it sends a request, with your IP address, to Google.

    Logs¶

    The service's log goes to the systemd journal on the host. It is used to operate the service and keep it secure. It records:

    • One line per request: method, path and query string, response status, response time, and the address the connection came from. The instance sits behind a Tailscale proxy on the same host, so that address is the proxy's (127.0.0.1), not yours.
    • Each sign-in, with your account id and email, and each sign-in refused by the admission rules, with the provider's account id and the email it gave.

    The journal is kept as long as journald keeps it. The host sets no retention of its own, so journald's defaults apply: the journal is bounded by size (at most 10% of its file system, and at most 4 GiB), not by age, and the oldest entries are deleted as space is needed.

    Backups¶

    cointoss backup never copies auth.db. The deployment's daily snapshot does copy it, as a separate file readable only by the service account (mode 600), so that accounts and approval decisions survive rebuilding or moving the instance. The copy holds everything auth.db holds, including session and token secrets, which woodglue stores as they are rather than hashed. Snapshots stay on the instance's host. They are kept for the last 7 days plus one for each of the last 4 weeks; snapshots labelled for a specific event, such as an upgrade, are kept until deleted by hand.

    Sharing¶

    Your data is not sold, and it is not shared with anyone.

    • Sign-in goes through the provider you choose, under its own privacy policy: Google or GitHub.
    • Traffic reaches the instance through the operator's tailnet. As a guest you have a Tailscale account in it, under Tailscale's privacy policy.

    Market data¶

    The prices and universes cointoss records come from CoinGecko. They are public market data and contain no personal data.

    Your choices¶

    • See what is stored about you: ask (see Contact), and you will be told what auth.db holds for your account.
    • Have your account removed: ask, and the account is disabled, which deletes all of its sessions and tokens at once, and then its record is deleted from auth.db. Copies in snapshots taken before then age out on the schedule above, except in labelled snapshots, which last until those are deleted.
    • Sign out: signing out deletes your session on the instance.
    • Revoke the app's access at the provider: in Google, under Third-party apps and services; in GitHub, under Settings, Applications, Authorized OAuth Apps. Revoking it at the provider does not end a session already open on the instance; ask to have your account removed for that.

    Contact¶

    Open an issue at https://github.com/walnutgeek/cointoss/issues. Issues are public, so do not put anything in one that you would not want published. To ask about your own account, open an issue saying so, without personal details, and the maintainer will reply there to arrange it.

    Changes¶

    When what the instance collects or keeps changes, this page changes with it, and its date is updated. Every change is in the git history linked at the top.

    This site¶

    This documentation site is hosted on GitHub Pages, under GitHub's privacy statement. It sets no cookies of its own and loads no fonts, analytics or trackers from other sites. The repository link in its header asks GitHub's API for the repository's latest release, stars and forks.
